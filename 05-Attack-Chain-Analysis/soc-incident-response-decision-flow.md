# 🚨 SOC Incident Response Decision Flow

This document outlines a structured decision-making process used by SOC Analysts to handle alerts efficiently and consistently under pressure.

---

## 🔍 Step 1 — Alert Received

An alert is generated by SIEM/EDR/IDS tools.

**Key rule:**
> Alert ≠ Attack. Investigation determines risk.

---

## 🧠 Step 2 — Initial Context Check

Gather basic information:

- Who is the user?
- Which system is affected?
- Internal or external source?
- Time of activity (business hours / off-hours)?
- What type of activity occurred?

---

## 🔎 Step 3 — Log Analysis

Check logs for:

- Repeated behavior or single event
- Related suspicious activity
- Privilege usage
- System modifications
- Network communication

---

## ⚖ Step 4 — Determine Risk Level

| Indicator | Risk |
|----------|------|
Single failed login | Low |
Success after multiple failures | Medium |
Persistence creation | High |
Repeated outbound connections | High |
Privilege escalation | High |

---

## 🔗 Step 5 — Correlate Events

Ask:

- Does this event link to previous alerts?
- Do we see an attack chain forming?

> Execution → Persistence → Lateral Movement → C2 → Impact


Multiple related signals = likely compromise.

---

## 📝 Step 6 — Decision

| Condition | Action |
|-----------|--------|
Normal behavior confirmed | Close as False Positive |
Suspicious but unclear | Monitor / Observe |
Attack indicators present | Escalate to SOC L2 |

---

## 📤 Step 7 — Documentation

Document clearly:

- What happened
- Evidence reviewed
- Why the decision was made
- Risk assessment

---

## 🔑 Core Principle

> Investigate first. Decide with evidence. Escalate when risk impacts business.
